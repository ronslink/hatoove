**Task:** task-45. **Branch:** `codex/content-rights-01` (worktree `.worktrees/content-rights`), base `c809737`
(local main, the commit that removed the gate). **Files:** `tools/content-rights-check.mjs`,
`tools/run-gates.mjs`, this note. No serving policy, migration or product file was changed — and the one time a
product file was touched it was a deliberate mutation that was restored byte-for-byte from a saved copy
(`git status --short server/` empty afterwards).

## Verdict: (a) — the check was wrong, the policy is correct

The leg never reached the rights filter. Its synthetic content rows could not resolve a **review** subject, so
they were excluded before rights were consulted. Measured, in order:

**1. The failure as inherited, reproduced on a freshly migrated disposable database:**

```text
PASS 1. every content row carries a recorded rights basis  [48 content row(s), every one with a recorded basis]
PASS 2. the decision records who decided, when, and why  [generated by Ron (product owner) on 2026-10-05]
PASS 3. the served catalogue reports the recorded basis, not "unknown"  [objective-sets[16] tasks[6] vocab[300] …]
PASS 4. a decision record cannot be updated or deleted  [UPDATE and DELETE both refused …]
FAIL 5. content with no rights basis is refused while generated content is served
     generated content must be served; got ["Wohnungen und WG-Zimmer","Stellenanzeigen und Arbeitsuche","Kurse und Freizeitangebote"]
PASS 6. the content rows still carry what was true when they were written  […]
```

The failure is on the leg's own **control**: the row that should be served is absent.

**2. Why it is absent — the projection refuses the subject.** Asking the projector directly about each probe
row, with the fixture exactly as delivered:

```text
generated -> {"review_status":"unavailable","review_basis":"none","blocked":true}
unknown   -> {"review_status":"unavailable","review_basis":"none","blocked":true}
missing   -> {"review_status":"unavailable","review_basis":"none","blocked":true}
```

Two independent defects in the fixture produce that, and either alone is fatal:

- **`kind='objective'` has no branch.** `resolve_review_subject` (`server/migrations/0035-content-review.sql:67`)
  resolves a content subject for `task`, `rubric`, `guide`, `lexicon` and `media` (`:76`–`:83`) and nothing
  else, so `ok` stays false and the function RETURNs no row.
- **`content_sha256='probe'` is not a digest.** `:84` RETURNs unless
  `content_sha256 ~ '^[0-9a-f]{64}$'`.

With no row from `project_content_review`, `effective_content_review` yields
`review_status='unavailable', blocked=true`, and the row is dropped by the review predicate
(`server/owned-postgres/adapter.mjs:442`, `c.review_status = ANY($3)`) and by the route's
`contentBlockReason` (`server/owned-api.mjs:1010`) — **before** the rights predicate at
`adapter.mjs:445` (`COALESCE(cr.basis, c.rights_status) = ANY($6::text[])`) is ever reached.

**3. The fixture shape that works, measured.** With `kind='task'` (the branch at `0035:76-77` that accepts a
content row backed by an `objective_set`) and a real 64-hex digest:

```text
generated -> {"review_status":"unreviewed","blocked":false,"served_rights":"generated"}
unknown   -> {"review_status":"unreviewed","blocked":false,"served_rights":"unknown"}
missing   -> {"review_status":"unreviewed","blocked":false,"served_rights":"unknown"}
licensed  -> {"review_status":"unreviewed","blocked":false,"served_rights":"licensed"}

served LV1 count: 4
titles: [["Probe generated","generated"],["Wohnungen und WG-Zimmer","generated"],
         ["Stellenanzeigen und Arbeitsuche","generated"],["Kurse und Freizeitangebote","generated"]]
direct unknown status: 404 | direct generated status: 200 | answers on unknown: 404
```

All four probe rows are now review-servable and the **only** thing separating them is the rights decision.
That is the invariant the leg was written for, and it now runs.

## The inherited diagnosis was wrong about the mechanism, and I am recording the correction

The hand-over said the catalogue is assembled by `readReleasedForm` (release head → release → form →
`exam_form_member` ⋈ `objective_set`) so "a bare `objective_set` insert is not filtered — it is not part of the
form at all". That is true of the **pinned-service route** (`readReleasedForm`,
`server/owned-postgres/packages.mjs:47`), not of `GET /api/v1/objective-sets`. The catalogue route uses
`listObjectiveSets` (`adapter.mjs:425`), which reads `objective_set` directly and admits membership only through
`importedSetGate()` (`packages.mjs:20-25`); that gate returns `true` when `count(*)=0` **and**
`source_path NOT LIKE 'content/exams/%'` — the fallback for actual historical practice content. So a probe row
with `source_path='probe'` needs no release, form or member row, and none was created. Measurement 3 above is
the evidence: the corrected row is served with no release fixture at all.

Consequence: **recipe 1 was not needed**, so no `exam_release_head` was touched and there is no `finally`
restore to get wrong. Nothing to leak.

**Recipe 2 is not available either**, which is worth knowing before anyone reaches for it again: the plan was to
plant an `unknown` basis on a set that already serves. `content_rights.content_version_id` is the **PRIMARY
KEY** (`server/migrations/0020-content-rights.sql:25`), the table is append-only — a `BEFORE UPDATE OR DELETE`
trigger refuses both (`:33`, asserted by leg 4) — and leg 1 requires every content row to have a decision. There
is therefore no way to give a *seeded* set an `unknown` basis, and no way to remove its recorded one.

## What the vacuous leg would have let through — demonstrated, not argued

The three negative asserts (`"Probe unknown"/"missing"/"licensed"` are absent) passed only because **no synthetic
row was served at all**. They were not testing rights. Proof, run as a mutation:

- Take the pre-fix leg verbatim from `c809737`, neutralize its **four control asserts** (the two that require the
  probe row to be served, and the two that require it to remain readable/widenable), and leave the three rights
  negatives exactly as shipped.
- Mutate the deployment so it **accepts `unknown`** (`known` gains `'unknown'`, and the default
  `B1PREP_SERVE_RIGHTS` becomes `'generated,unknown'`).
- Result:

```text
PASS 5. content with no rights basis is refused while generated content is served
       [served ["Wohnungen und WG-Zimmer","Stellenanzeigen und Arbeitsuche","Kurse und Freizeitangebote"]
        — "Probe unknown" is absent]
1 passed, 0 pending, 0 failed
```

`exit=0`. The leg prints "Probe unknown is absent" **while the deployment serves it**. The repaired leg fails
that same mutation by name (M-C below).

Honest nuance: as delivered the old leg was **red**, on its control, so it was not at that moment hiding a live
defect. What it lacked was any discriminating power on the rights question — so the very next person to "make the
control pass" (by giving the probe rows a resolvable shape, which is what this lease did) would have turned a red
gate green for the wrong reason, and the **POOL-01 defect it exists to catch** — a content row with no rights
decision being invisible to a fail-closed policy while every row count and text check stays green — could have
recurred behind it unnoticed.

## The repair

No assert was weakened. Three were added, and the fixture was given the shape the domain requires.

1. **`kind='task'` + a real 64-hex `content_sha256`**, with the reason written into the code as a comment: this
   is a fixture requirement of `resolve_review_subject`, not decoration.
2. **The anti-vacuity guard (new).** For every probe row, before any catalogue call:
   `effective_content_review` must resolve (`blocked=false`, review status `approved`/`unreviewed`) **and**
   `COALESCE(cr.basis, c.rights_status)` must equal the basis under test. This is the assert the old leg lacked:
   if a probe row stops being review-servable, the leg now fails **by name** instead of the negatives passing by
   accident.
3. **A real control (new).** The seeded LV1 sets are captured before the probe rows exist and asserted still
   served afterwards, so the leg proves the catalogue works, not only that it refuses. The synthetic
   `generated` row is the second control.
4. **A no-leak assert (new).** No served row may report a basis the deployment has not accepted.
5. **Leg 6 scoped to seeded rows** (`content_version_id NOT LIKE 'rights-probe-%'`): its claim is about real
   content's seed-time fact, and the probe rows are also `kind='task'`. Nothing was weakened; the claim is now
   about what it says it is about.

Unchanged and still strict: the direct-URL 404 for the withheld set, the 200 for the accepted one, the 404 on
`POST …/answers` before key lookup, and the "an operator cannot widen `unknown` or absent provenance in" assert
(the policy's allow-list is filtered through `known = {generated, licensed, commissioned}`).

## Not verified, and the residual risk

- **The default `public` content mode is still covered elsewhere**, not by this check: the check sets
  `B1PREP_CONTENT_MODE='internal-preview'` deliberately (the seeded rows are `unreviewed`) and says so at the
  top. A rights regression under `public` would be caught by the same query path but is not this leg's claim.
- **The refusal is proven at the observable level, not per layer.** Two layers can refuse a row — the SQL
  predicate (`adapter.mjs:445`) and the route filter (`owned-api.mjs:1010`). The leg asserts the *outcome*, so a
  change that removes one layer stays green while the other still refuses. That is correct behaviour
  (defence in depth), but this leg is not a unit test of either layer. I verified by hand that removing only the
  SQL predicate does **not** change the observable result, which is why M-C mutates the policy instead.
- **There is still no way to correct or withdraw a wrong decision.** `content_rights` is append-only with one row
  per content row, so a mistaken `generated` decision is permanent. That is a deliberate design property
  ("an audit trail, not a switch"), and it also means a re-granting path does not exist. Out of scope here; it
  is a decision for whoever needs it first.
- **Leg 5 leaves four probe rows in the fixture schema.** `createFixture` creates a random `ownapi_<hex>` schema
  and drops it in `cleanup()`, so nothing survives a run; within a run, leg 6 is scoped away from them.
- **The other five `mirror-db` gates were run as a group, not inspected.** They were green before and after; I
  did not read their internals.
