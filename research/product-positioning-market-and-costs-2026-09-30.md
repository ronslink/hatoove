# B1 preparation product positioning and launch economics

Research checked 30 September 2026. This memo revisits the production strategy on D: and the two supporting reports on G:. It recommends a small direct-to-learner paid pilot of a standalone, self-service exam-preparation product. Learners must be able to choose, buy and use it independently; school membership, a teacher or a class is not required. Direct AVGS reimbursement should contribute zero revenue in the launch model. A profitable specialist business is plausible; demand, grading quality and willingness to pay remain unproven.

**Current scope supersedes the earlier speaking-led proposal:** launch preparation for listening, reading, language elements where the selected exam includes them, and writing. Speaking practice and oral assessment are deferred following the user's experience with inconsistent speech features. Listening uses fixed, reviewed recordings; learners do not record or upload speech. Native-language instructions and explanations support exam preparation, while exam tasks remain in the target language. This is preparation for the non-oral sections, not a complete exam simulation or a whole-exam pass predictor.

**Current business model supersedes the earlier school-led proposal:** help learners who already have the required language foundation practise the format and tasks of a specific exam. This is not a language course or a classroom-management product. Schools may become an optional referral or purchasing channel later; they are not a product dependency or a launch revenue assumption.

## Recommended product and customer

Proposed promise: **Prepare independently for the listening and written sections of your B1 exam, understand your mistakes, and focus your practice before exam day.** Lead with consistent listening and reading practice, language elements where applicable, and specific writing feedback. Reviewed answer keys, fixed audio, native-language explanations and personal section progress support that promise. Keep all exercises tied to the selected exam rather than expanding into a general language course.

Start with independent learners who already have roughly the target B1 language foundation and an exam planned in four to eight weeks, including repeat candidates. The learner is the primary buyer and decides when and what to practise. Do not promise to teach German from the beginning or to move a beginner to B1 within the pass period. A diagnostic can identify weak exam sections and indicate that further language study may be needed; it cannot certify an overall CEFR level. Course participation obligations and benefit receipt do not establish an individual's willingness to pay; interview actual learners across the intended purchasing regions.

Core self-service journey: **choose a specific exam and exam date → complete a diagnostic for the supported sections → follow focused practice and revision → review section progress and unseen timed practice.** Let learners choose a target date if their booking is pending and adjust the plan themselves. Explain the selected format, timing, task types, mistakes and next practice in their chosen instruction language. Keep exam stimulus and response requirements in the exam language. Account creation, access, practice, feedback and progress must work without teacher assignment or school approval.

Use an independent brand that can eventually support telc Deutsch B1, DTZ and Goethe B1. For the first paid alpha, correct and validate the existing **telc Deutsch B1** module. Add a dedicated DTZ module when direct learner demand is demonstrated, then Goethe. If early paying learners instead need DTZ, build and validate that format before accepting purchases for it. The documented DTZ population is an expansion market, not the launch market for a telc-only product.

These examinations share a language level, not a blueprint. Keep their supported tasks, section timing, grading and content versions separate. The existing [exam review](../exam-product-review.md) identifies material writing and speaking mismatches; correct the writing and other supported sections before release, while treating its oral findings as deferred work. Recheck the current implementation against the official model, including whether language elements are a separate section. Whole-exam pass rules remain reference information and must not be applied to an incomplete non-oral practice result. [telc format](https://www.telc.net/sprachpruefungen/deutsch/zertifikat-deutsch-telc-deutsch-b1/), [Goethe format](https://www.goethe.de/ins/de/de/m/prf/prf/gzb1/inf.html).

## Competition changes the promise

The earlier assertion that nobody combines AI writing and speaking feedback is untenable. The comparison below preserves the sourced competitive research; its speaking capabilities are market context, not the current product's launch scope. These are advertised capabilities and prices checked on the research date; this research did not test their quality or establish customer traction.

| Product | Advertised offer | Listed price |
|---|---|---|
| [LevelKraft](https://levelkraft.de/) | Exam practice, speaking and writing AI feedback, personalised learning plan | €7.99 monthly or €17.99 for three months, including VAT |
| [PassDeutsch](https://passdeutsch.com/) | Goethe, telc and DTZ preparation; model tests and AI writing and speaking feedback | Homepage advertises free access |
| [telc Online Schreibtraining](https://shop.telc.net/de_DE/online-schreibtraining-deutsch-b1.html) | B1 writing course with criterion-level telc AI feedback | €24.50 for one year |
| [vhs Lernportal](https://b1.vhs-lernportal.de/wws/4054986.php) | B1 self-study course and apps | Free |

The opportunity is to make independent exam preparation clear, consistent and easy to act on. Proposed differentiators are examiner-reviewed tasks, consistent audio and answer keys, writing feedback benchmarked against independent human ratings, native-language explanations, and clear next exercises matched to the learner's exam date and weak sections. Independent teachers or examiners contribute to internal content review and calibration; users do not need their own teacher. These are development and validation targets, not claims the current application can already make. The narrower offer must prove its value against free practice materials and writing-focused competitors.

**Superseded oral recommendation:** the earlier proposal to launch speaking with formative AI feedback is no longer active. Transcript-only feedback cannot establish pronunciation, intonation or natural interaction, and transcription may normalise learner mistakes. Any future oral module needs a separate quality and cost evaluation. DTZ makes B1 speaking essential to the overall B1 result, so performance in this app's supported sections cannot establish complete DTZ readiness. [DTZ scoring and format](https://www.gast.de/de/forschung-entwicklung/entwicklung/auftraege/deutsch-test-fuer-zuwanderer-dtz/der-dtz-auf-einen-blick), [DTZ pass rule](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/inttestv/__10.html).

Do not advertise a full mock exam, complete readiness score or whole-exam pass probability while oral assessment is absent. Show section-specific practice results, writing estimates, coverage and performance on unseen timed tasks. Deterministic answer scoring can be consistent without making AI writing feedback infallible. The absence of a specialist product from a large language-app company does not establish market size or prove an opportunity.

The strongest domestic anchor is **318,550 DTZ participants in 2025**. This includes 51,132 people with a repeat procedure; adding them again double-counts. The statistic retains each person's highest result, so its 55.0% B1 result rate is not a first-attempt pass rate. [Government answer, pages 8–9](https://dserver.bundestag.de/btd/21/057/2105716.pdf).

Goethe now publishes **461,000 B1 examinations worldwide in 2025**. This corrects the earlier claim that no B1 breakdown was available. It is not a Germany-only count of unique buyers, and module/repeat counting requires clarification before combining it with other data. Domestic telc B1 volume remains unverified. [Goethe examination statistics](https://www.goethe.de/de/spr/prf.html).

The retail ceilings are hypothetical, not forecasts or evidence that all participants need or can buy this app. Their €29/€39 prices came from the broader bundle proposal and are retained only as price sensitivities; willingness to pay for the narrower non-oral offer must be retested. At €39 including an assumed 19% VAT, revenue is €32.77 per sale. Do not mix consumer receipts, net revenue, school licence prices, annual subscriptions and exam passes in one market model.

Naturalisation figures must not be added as a separate annual exam cohort: applicants may reuse existing qualifications or qualify through other evidence. Likewise, course entrants, exam attempts and certificates are different units. Do not add worldwide Goethe exams to domestic DTZ participants and label the sum German customers.

For business planning, replace a claimed market-share forecast with an observed direct-purchase funnel. Track eligible visitors who select a supported exam and date, diagnostic completion, pass purchase and subsequent practice. Do not assign a conversion rate or CAC until measured. The earlier school/cohort revenue model is superseded and excluded from the current launch case.

## Funding and procurement

The following retains the funding research for context. The standalone launch assumes ordinary consumer purchases, no funding entitlement and no institutional procurement dependency.

BA guidance effective **10 September 2026** expressly excludes exam preparation, simulations and language examinations, including DTZ examples, from §45 activation measures. General German is outside BA funding responsibility. Bildungsgutschein is a separate §81 instrument for vocational further training; it is not a general app voucher. [BA implementation guidance 2/2026](https://www.arbeitsagentur.de/datei/umsetzungshinweis-02-2026_ba059832.pdf), [Bildungsgutschein](https://www.arbeitsagentur.de/karriere-und-weiterbildung/bildungsgutschein).

A school could still buy or refer users to the standalone product through a future optional channel. Such a channel would need its own purchasing budget and contractual permissions; purchase by an approved provider does not automatically establish reimbursement. BA guidance permitting asynchronous learning costs inside a measure does not override substantive funding exclusions. There is no verified entitlement to additional BAMF reimbursement for this product. [BA implementation guidance 1/2026](https://www.arbeitsagentur.de/datei/umsetzungshinweis-01-2026_ba055638.pdf).

Do not invest in AZAV certification as a shortcut to the proposed exam-prep funding route. Validate direct learner purchases first. Also, g.a.s.t. has administered DTZ since January 2023; treating the entire DTZ channel as controlled by telc is inaccurate. [g.a.s.t.](https://www.gast.de/de/).

The **€117.11** figure is a BAMF reimbursement amount for eligible DTZ participation, not a universal learner expense. Berlin Pankow lists funded participation at €0 and unfunded participation at €117.11. Price against demonstrated benefit and alternatives. [BAMF billing rules](https://www.bamf.de/SharedDocs/Anlagen/DE/Integration/Integrationskurse/Kurstraeger/Abrechnung/Richtlinien/richtlinien-abrechnung-stand-20250701.pdf?__blob=publicationFile&v=2), [Pankow programme](https://www.berlin.de/vhs/_assets/pankow/dokumente/programmheft-magazin-2026-1.pdf?ts=1764793577).

## Offer and distribution to test

Offer a self-service, fixed-duration pass for the selected exam's supported listening and written sections, with a free section diagnostic and one sample writing-feedback session. Match access to the exam-preparation cycle; make any paid extension for a changed date a separate, clear choice. **The former €29 versus €39 eight-week experiment priced a broader bundle and must be retested; neither price is automatically adopted.** Test a clear writing-review allowance, such as 20 submissions per eight-week pass, alongside repeat access to the reviewed listening, reading and applicable language-elements bank. The allowance is a hypothesis to validate against practice needs and usage. The pass contains no learner speech recording or assessment. Human writing review could be an optional, separately priced service later; it must not be necessary to use the core product.

Test affordability by purchasing region, independently of the language used for instructions. Native-language explanations do not establish a learner's purchasing power. Keep exam coverage and feedback standards consistent, and evaluate regional prices after taxes, payment fees and support. Include referral commissions only where a real agreement creates them. A small free diagnostic and optional sponsored passes can improve access without unlimited free AI feedback; sponsorship must not create a school-account or class requirement.

The earlier provider-seat offer and teacher dashboard are superseded launch ideas. School referrals or bulk gift-pass purchases may be explored later without changing the learner's independent experience. Do not build teacher assignments, class rosters, institutional approvals or a school dashboard as prerequisites for launch, and do not sell the product primarily on teacher-hours saved.

Recruit near-exam learners directly through exam-specific demonstrations, relevant learner communities, useful preparation pages and learner referrals. The landing page should identify the exact supported exam, required language foundation, included sections, deferred speaking scope, instruction languages, pass duration and price. Let the free diagnostic demonstrate the next practical step before purchase. Measure each channel separately, including founder effort; organic acquisition is not costless. Paid-search CAC and keyword volume remain unknown, so paid acquisition has no assumed contribution in the base launch plan.

## Cost model

Verified public rates, rechecked for this scope revision: Mistral Small 4 is **$0.15 input and $0.60 output per million tokens**, with a 10% regional inference premium. The active model covers text-based writing feedback and explanations. There is no transcription, learner-recording or oral-assessment usage in the launch budget. [Mistral prices](https://docs.mistral.ai/inference/pricing), [regional inference](https://docs.mistral.ai/inference/regional-inference).

Use this reproducible text-only planning formula, including the regional premium and no caching discount:

`Text AI dollars per month = 1.10 × (0.15 × input tokens / 1,000,000 + 0.60 × output tokens / 1,000,000)`

`Text AI budget with retry/variance reserve = text AI dollars × 1.25`

| Assumed monthly use | Input tokens | Output tokens | Text AI including regional premium | With 25% reserve |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Light | 20,000 | 4,000 | **$0.005940** | **$0.007425** |
| Engaged | 100,000 | 20,000 | **$0.029700** | **$0.037125** |
| Heavy | 300,000 | 60,000 | **$0.089100** | **$0.111375** |

These are illustrative token allocations, not measured learner demand or a claim that feedback accuracy has been established at this model price. All model output, including reasoning where billed, must fit the budget. They exclude fixed listening-content production and delivery, platform services, human review and support. A higher-quality model or additional grading passes may change the cost. Earlier speech-inclusive per-learner figures describe the superseded scope and must not be used as the current cost model.

For planning, assume every active learner consumes the engaged allocation and add 25% for retries and variance:

| Monthly active learners | Text AI with 25% reserve | Assumed app hosting and services budget | AI plus app/services subtotal per month |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 100 | $3.71 | $20–50 | **$23.71–53.71** |
| 1,000 | $37.13 | $50–150 | **$87.13–187.13** |
| 10,000 | $371.25 | $150–500 | **$521.25–871.25** |

Hosting ranges are planning allowances, not quotations or load-tested requirements for the eventual managed stack. The displayed subtotals use unrounded per-learner AI calculations and round only the results to cents. They are **not total application or business costs**: separately budget listening-library production, storage and audio delivery, development, content maintenance, staff, customer acquisition, payment fees, tax and legal review. A chosen managed platform may require a different hosting allowance. The previous $552 speech-inclusive estimate used a different feature mix and only 50% engagement; numerical similarity to a new subtotal does not validate either forecast. MAU is also not annual paying customers: an eight-week pass creates a different relationship between sales and active learners.

Produce listening audio from reviewed, authored scripts and publish versioned recordings with checked answer keys. Everyone attempting the same exercise version hears the same recording under that section's playback rules. This is fixed content production, not per-attempt AI generation or learner speech processing. At Mistral's listed TTS rate of $16 per million characters, **1.2m characters costs $19.20** before any applicable regional premium and revisions. Google lists WaveNet/Standard at $4 per million characters after the free allowance, making the same volume **$4.80 at the paid rate**; Neural2 is the $16 tier. Choose voices by listening-task quality and contractual fit, not synthesis price alone. These are synthesis bills, not the cost of a validated library. An illustrative 300 exercises × 15 review minutes × €40/hour already costs €3,000 in review labour. [Mistral TTS pricing](https://docs.mistral.ai/inference/pricing), [Google TTS pricing](https://cloud.google.com/text-to-speech/pricing).

Account for authored listening production as a shared content cost and for storage, requests and delivered audio bytes as ongoing delivery costs. Measure replays and cached delivery during the pilot; do not multiply a one-off synthesis bill by learner count or assume repeat playback is free. Keep these amounts separate from the text-token tables above.

The former bundle's €39 retail example yields €32.77 before costs under the assumed 19% VAT treatment; standard EEA Stripe processing is approximately €0.84. This is a retained arithmetic example, not adoption of €39 for the narrower offer. For each tested region and channel, calculate contribution as collected price less taxes remitted, payment/FX fees, refunds/chargebacks, partner commissions, text AI, listening delivery and variable support or human-writing-review costs. Then check whether realistic sales cover shared content production, grading validation, platform operations and maintenance. Low token cost alone does not establish a viable price or margin. Paid-search CAC remains unverified. [Stripe Germany fees](https://stripe.com/de/pricing).

## Economical implementation

Keep the existing lightweight web interface and useful learning logic where they survive the content audit. Build one mobile-friendly web application with an EU-region backend, database, managed authentication, backups and storage for the authored listening library. Implement direct signup, exam/date selection, diagnostic, checkout/pass entitlement, independent practice and personal section progress as one complete journey. No school tenant, teacher assignment or class enrollment is required. The supported learner inputs are written answers and exercise selections; there is no microphone capture or learner-audio upload path. Choose a small operationally manageable stack before trying to minimise its monthly bill. Free tiers and a cheap virtual machine establish a compute floor; they do not establish a complete service budget.

Use reviewed tasks and deterministic scoring for listening, reading and language elements where applicable. Serve fixed authored audio, test playback across supported devices and browsers, and enforce the selected section's timing and replay rules in timed practice. For writing, request bounded, structured formative feedback against the selected exam's rubric and retain the rubric version with the attempt. Native-language explanations should be reviewed for clarity without translating away the language skill being tested. Present results only for the sections practised, with writing estimates distinguished from answer-key scores.

Add account isolation, writing-submission quotas, server-side model allowlists, text-length limits, cost telemetry, retry limits, content versions and durable deletion. The local app's shared progress file and configuration endpoints require a service redesign before public access. Preserve each historical attempt's original rubric. Keep content administration and independent expert review internal, separate from learner access. Defer speaking practice, STT, acoustic assessment, live voice infrastructure, native mobile apps, self-hosted models, three simultaneous exam launches, teacher dashboards and school integrations.

Listening playback remains in scope; browser SpeechRecognition, microphone permission, recording controls and pronunciation scoring do not. Do not use dynamic browser TTS to generate the assessed listening exercise at runtime. Retaining any spoken feature from the older application would require a later explicit scope decision and its own evaluation.

## Data and education requirements

An EU company name is not an EU processing guarantee. Mistral's global endpoint lacks a regional commitment; its EU endpoint describes EU/EFTA processing and exceptions for operational metadata. Verify the chosen model, geography, DPA, subprocessors, training settings and retention. Zero data retention is a separate approval-based control. [Regional inference](https://docs.mistral.ai/inference/regional-inference), [Mistral retention controls](https://docs.mistral.ai/admin/monitor-comply/zero-data-retention).

Berlin's official DeepSeek app-store notification was **27 June 2025**, not 23 June 2026. It substantiates transfer concerns for that service, not a blanket prohibition on every deployment of its model weights. Select a contracted service suitable for learner data in the direct-to-consumer product. [Berlin DPA notice](https://www.datenschutz-berlin.de/pressemitteilung/berliner-datenschutzbeauftragte-meldet-ki-app-deepseek-in-deutschland-bei-apple-und-google-als-rechtswidrigen-inhalt).

Article 50 transparency obligations have applied since 2 August 2026; the Commission reports Annex III high-risk provisions applying from 2 December 2027. Provider marking and interaction disclosures are distinct requirements. Educational learning-outcome assessment and steering can raise high-risk classification questions; a practice label alone is insufficient. Assess the actual self-service intended use before public release rather than assuming that the absence of a school settles classification. [Commission enforcement dates](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/faq/when-does-enforcement-start), [Commission AI Act FAQ](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/navigating-ai-act), [Article 50](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-50).

Real health or religious disclosures in written answers can invoke GDPR Article 9. Fictional role-play is not automatically personal health data. Use invented scenario details, minimise identification and written-response retention, and establish appropriate grounds for any real sensitive data. Removing learner-audio collection reduces one data flow but does not remove the sensitive-data risk in exam-style letters. [EDPB lawful processing guidance](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/sme/be-compliant/process-personal-data-lawfully_en).

The product is positioned as self-service exam practice, not a language course. Obtain a specific **FernUSG/ZFU assessment of the actual paid offer** rather than assuming that this label or the absence of a teacher establishes an exemption. The ZFU's criteria do not conclusively settle AI-only feedback. Also confirm consumer terms, applicable VAT treatment and rights to all content and branding. [ZFU criteria](https://zfu.de/veranstaltende/fernunterricht), [ZFU FAQ](https://zfu.de/veranstaltende/faq).

## Proposed six week validation programme

1. **Weeks 1–2:** Interview fifteen to twenty independent near-exam learners with an existing language foundation, including repeat candidates and the intended purchasing regions. Compare the supported sections with current competitors using the same original tasks. Test the exam/date selection and free diagnostic without facilitator help, then test a concrete pass offer and willingness to pay. Distinguish stated interest from actual purchases. Select the first exam from this evidence and make the omitted speaking component clear before purchase. Engage qualified independent reviewers for content and scoring quality; they are product specialists, not required teachers for users.
2. **Weeks 2–4:** Correct that exam's supported blueprint; author a small reviewed bank with fixed listening recordings and independently checked answer keys; implement the safe multi-user pilot and instrument text tokens, retries, audio delivery and support. Build an initial evaluation set of 100–200 consented writing samples, independently rated by two qualified raters. Keep held-out samples separate from prompt tuning. Measure false reassurance near writing-section thresholds and differences across proficiency and first languages. Separately check listening playback, timing and replay rules across supported devices and browsers, and review native-language explanations. This sample exposes defects; it does not alone prove broad validity.
3. **Weeks 4–6:** Recruit 50–100 independent learners into a self-service pilot with a clearly priced pass experiment. Group analysis by supported exam, exam date, purchasing region and instruction language; these are measurement cohorts, not classes or institutional enrollment. Track diagnostic completion, diagnostic-to-purchase conversion, successful checkout, first practice completion, return practice before the exam, support needs and refunds. Compare initial results with unseen follow-up listening, reading, applicable language-elements and writing tasks. Record acquisition spending and effort by channel without projecting a CAC from tiny samples. Compare with usual practice where practical; selected pilot improvements alone do not establish causal exam-pass gains or readiness in the omitted oral section.

Proposed continuation criteria: independent learners can complete the core journey without a teacher or founder walkthrough; the priced offer produces direct purchases; purchasers practise again before their exam; answer keys and listening playback are dependable; writing feedback has no unresolved serious false reassurance; and pass contribution plus expected volume can support delivery and shared operating costs. Set minimum purchase, conversion, return-practice and refund criteria before reviewing the paid experiment, using an explicitly stated sample and acquisition budget rather than invented market benchmarks. Agree numeric writing-grading tolerances with the raters before scoring the held-out set. If learners complete free diagnostics but will not buy, revise the value, package or price before expanding content or advertising. Annual retention and repeat school orders are not success requirements for a time-limited exam product; completion around the exam date can be the intended end of use. Speaking is not a launch gate or a promised follow-up feature in this programme.

Allocate the next spending to independent expert review, measured feedback quality, a complete self-service journey and tests with paying individual learners. The research supports inexpensive delivery; only the pilot can establish a viable standalone exam-preparation offer.
